# Your AI & ML hiring portfolio toolkit

Research prepared 7 November 2026; edition 1.1.0 published 8 November 2026. Designed for learners starting from zero, with a six-hour study-day planning budget and a global job-search goal.

## Open the guide

- Open `downloads/AI-ML-Hiring-Portfolio-Plan.html` in a browser. Its styling, navigation and content are self-contained; no installation, account, external font or API key is needed. Source links need internet access.
- Use the PDF for reading/printing, or the Markdown for editing.
- The HTML includes 15 sections, 20 reviewed job descriptions with qualification boundaries, and 25 linked source pages. The job sample is purposive and technology-company-heavy, not a global census or a list of confirmed current vacancies.

Use **Build your portfolio plan** near the top to choose a role focus, adjust study time and export a Markdown checklist. Saving is optional and local to your browser; exported plans can move between devices. The calendar rescales an authored 864-hour allowance, and the checks record self-assessment. Neither establishes competence or predicts hiring.

## Start with a program you can own

1. Read sections 1, 4 and 15. Build a tiny CSV checker before starting either flagship.
2. Copy `templates/project-readme.md` into your learning project and replace only fields you can substantiate.
3. Use `templates/milestones.csv` to track evidence gates. Extend the dates when a gate is unmet.

The recommended sequence is a small CSV tool → Demand Replay → Evidence Desk → a reliability release inside one flagship → a useful contribution and an evidence-based application loop. Section 3 gives the decision process for changing that sequence when your eligible target roles differ.

## Use the templates as work records

The `templates/` folder contains a README, evaluation plan, experiment log, case study, publishing checklist/post drafts, application tracker, milestone sheet and results CSV. They are **unfilled templates**, not records of completed evaluations or applications. Keep personal application notes private. Never replace “not measured” with an intended target score.

For sharing or participating, see [announcement drafts](docs/SHARE-KIT.md), [contribution ideas](CONTRIBUTING.md), and the [community showcase invitation](docs/PROJECT-SHOWCASE.md).

## Research records and limits

- `evidence/source-ledger.json`: 25 source URLs, access dates, scope notes and capture hashes.
- `evidence/reviewed-jobs.json`: 20 analytical records, short attributed excerpts, eligibility boundaries, source warnings and provenance. H17 retains a warning about unfinished employer templates; it is excluded from sponsorship/remote eligibility conclusions.
- `evidence/dataset-feasibility.json`: actual inspection of the official Bike Sharing archive. No model was trained and no performance result was established.
- `evidence/build-validation.json` and `evidence/browser-checks.json`: checks performed on the generated guide.

Raw full employer feeds, full source-page captures, the downloaded dataset and build dependencies are deliberately not redistributed in this compact toolkit. Capture paths in ledgers identify the retained research workspace files; they are not promised bundle members. Third-party quotations and source materials retain their respective rights. The dataset and Python documentation have their own licenses, explained in the guide.

The time allowances are planning estimates after prerequisites. The 24-week schedule includes learning and review at six focused hours on six days each week; it is not a deadline or a hiring guarantee. The guide proposes portfolio projects and activities; it does not claim that the publisher has completed those projects, achieved model scores or obtained employment through them.
